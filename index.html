03 — Problem Solving in Action

When there is
no obvious answer.

Real situations where I had to step in, take ownership and figure out what needed to happen next.

CASE 01
The concert that wasn't supposed to happen
Crisis Problem Solving

The situation

A band from Estonia had a concert in Israel when the security situation suddenly changed and flights from Europe to Israel were being cancelled.

The challenge

The band needed to get to Israel, but there was no single route or simple solution. Flights were disappearing and plans were changing.

My response

I started solving it person by person. I found alternative flights, rebuilt routes as availability changed and kept coordinating the group.

When things changed again

Someone became stranded in Warsaw. Another route had to go through Greece. Each new problem meant finding a new solution.

The result Within roughly 24 hours, I had rebuilt the travel plan and got almost the entire band to Israel. The concert happened.
CASE 02
Taking over when everything changed
Leadership Under Pressure

The situation

An unexpected leadership crisis put the organisation under significant pressure. I was away on my days off when the situation emerged and returned immediately.

The challenge

There was no established playbook. Senior stakeholders from different countries needed to be brought together across leadership, ownership, banking, legal and finance.

My response

I stepped into the coordination role, identifying who needed to be involved and bringing international stakeholders together to establish what needed to happen, when and by whom.

The human side

Alongside the business response, I worked toward a constructive transition and tried to handle the situation as humanely as possible.

What it taught me When there is no clear plan, I don't wait for one to appear. I start building one.
CASE 03
Turning hundreds of people into one coordinated effort
Purpose & Coordination

The situation

Through LeMonID and project engagements, I organised the social-impact track of Wise Summer Days across multiple years.

The wider event brought together more than 1,000 people, with hundreds choosing to contribute to different social-impact projects.

The challenge

Participants were distributed across multiple organisations and projects, from community and renovation work to activities with children and environmental projects.

For Mission Days 2020, Wise also approached me directly to help with the social-impact element of their internal conference taking place across Tallinn, Budapest and Singapore.

My responsibility

I coordinated organisations, team leads, participants, transportation, schedules, materials and the practical details required to make the projects work.

Beyond the social track

During Summer Days I also worked with the wider operational infrastructure behind the event.

The recurring challenge was turning a large number of people, organisations and moving parts into one coordinated outcome.

The bigger picture What made these projects meaningful to me was that the problem solving had a purpose. I wasn't just organising people and logistics — I was helping create a situation where people could contribute to something bigger than themselves while organisations benefited from their work.
CASE 04
Organising and leading people around a common purpose
People & Purpose

The situation

I helped organise and lead a team behind a large public demonstration in Tallinn, bringing people together around a shared concern and common goal.

The challenge

Turning a shared idea into something real requires more than people agreeing with it. Responsibilities need to be coordinated and someone needs to keep the moving parts connected.

My role

I helped organise and lead the team, coordinating people and practical work needed to turn the idea into an actual public demonstration.

Why it matters to me

It demonstrated something I value deeply: people with different backgrounds coming together because they believe something is worth achieving.

The transferable lesson The common goal doesn't have to be a protest. It can be a company, a project, a team or an operational problem. What motivates me is bringing people together, building trust and helping them work toward something that matters.
CASE 05
The December 31st shipment
Time-Critical Operations

The situation

A year-end shipment was at risk. The container was stuck, the shipping company was experiencing problems and the bill of lading was not going to be ready in time.

The challenge

The material needed to leave within the current year. Waiting until January wasn't an acceptable outcome.

My response

I identified the people who could influence each part of the problem, contacted the right parties and coordinated the moving pieces until we found a way forward.

The bigger picture

Month-end and especially year-end were recurring periods of intense operational pressure, requiring quick decisions and constant coordination.

The result I was able to resolve these kinds of issues and keep shipments moving toward the required month-end or year-end deadline.

04 — What I'm looking for

I don't just want
a job.

I want a reason to care.

I've spent much of my career solving problems, coordinating people and making complicated things work.

What I've learned is that the work itself doesn't have to be extraordinary for the outcome to be meaningful.

A cause doesn't have to be a charity. Purpose doesn't have to mean social impact. And a common goal doesn't have to mean an event.

It can exist in almost any business, in almost any role.

It can be a company solving a real problem for its customers. It can be an operations team making people's lives easier. It can be a group of people building something they genuinely believe should exist.

Even ordinary or “boring” work becomes meaningful when you understand who benefits from it, why it matters and what you're working toward together.

How I work best

I don't need a clearly defined role.
I need a clearly defined outcome.

Give me the destination. I'll figure out the route.

Give me a problem, an objective, a team that needs direction, or a situation that has become complicated. I don't need every step mapped out before I start.

I need to understand what we're trying to achieve — then I'll figure out what needs to happen.

That doesn't mean I need chaos or that I can't work within structure. It means I don't want to be limited to a narrow box when the problem clearly crosses several.

Startup

The company is taking off and the next big move needs someone to push it from idea into reality.

Growing company

Momentum is there, but processes, people and responsibilities need to catch up.

Large corporation

A complicated problem sits between teams or departments and needs someone to connect the pieces and take ownership.

Team leadership

There is a goal, a group of people and a need to build trust, direction and a team that genuinely wants to achieve it.

I can work in a startup, a scale-up or a large international corporation.

I just don't want to sit inside a box while the real problem sits somewhere else.
Where I think I can be especially useful

At the point where something needs to move.

Sometimes that's a startup preparing for its next big leap. Sometimes it's a large organisation trying to solve something that doesn't fit neatly into one department.

At a growing company, that might mean entering a new market, building the right team, fixing an operational mess or pushing the next major initiative.

Inside a large organisation, it might mean connecting teams, navigating stakeholders, fixing gaps between departments or taking ownership of a problem that has gradually become everyone's and no one's responsibility.

Structure and autonomy aren't opposites. I can work inside an established organisation and still want the freedom to figure out the best way to reach the outcome.

The size of the company matters less to me than the room to take ownership.
And ultimately, I want the people around me to care.

I want to be part of something where people are working toward a common goal, rather than simply completing tasks.

Where I can bring people together, build trust and help a team become something more than a collection of individuals.

Where solving a difficult problem isn't just about getting it off someone's desk — it's about creating a better outcome for someone.

That's the part of work that gives me energy.

Social impact

Sometimes the purpose is directly helping people, communities or causes.

Building something

Sometimes it's creating a product, service or company that genuinely makes something better.

Making things work

Sometimes the impact is less visible — improving a broken process, supporting customers or making people's work easier.

Building the team

Sometimes the greatest impact is creating a team where people trust each other, care about the goal and want to give their best.

Give me a difficult problem.
Give me good people.
Give us something worth achieving.
I'll care about making it happen.
Another way to work together
It doesn't have to be a full-time job.

I'm also open to working on a project or service basis.

I have my own companies through which I can provide services and invoice clients. If you have a difficult project, an operational problem, a transition that needs someone to take ownership of, or a situation where you simply need someone to figure things out — I can step in.

Sometimes you need another permanent person.

Sometimes you need someone who can walk into the mess, take ownership and make it work.

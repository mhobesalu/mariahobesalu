03 — Problem Solving in Action

These are a few examples of situations where I had to step in, take ownership and figure out what needed to happen next.

CASE 01
The December 31st shipment
Crisis Management

The situation

A year-end shipment was at risk. The container was stuck, the shipping company was experiencing problems and the bill of lading was not going to be ready in time.

The challenge

The material needed to leave within the current year. Waiting until January wasn't an acceptable outcome.

My response

I identified the people who could influence each part of the problem, contacted the right parties and coordinated the moving pieces until we found a way forward.

What it shows

When a deadline is immovable and the normal process breaks down, I find the people who can change the outcome and start moving.

The result In these recurring month-end and year-end situations, I was able to resolve the issues and keep shipments moving toward the required deadline.
CASE 02
300 people, multiple organisations & whatever went wrong next
Large-Scale Coordination

The situation

During Wise Summer Days in Tartu, around 1,700 people took part in several activity tracks. I managed the Social Track, involving around 300 participants across numerous organisations.

The challenge

I coordinated organisations, team leads, participants, buses, schedules and materials. At the same time I managed the Co-Team handling event infrastructure.

My response

I became the coordination point when unexpected issues appeared, identifying who needed to be involved and bringing the right people together to solve them.

An example

When a train developed blocked toilets during the event, we worked out how to get the right emergency service involved to pump them empty.

The result A large, multi-location social programme and its operational infrastructure continued to function despite a constant stream of unexpected problems.
CASE 03
Making international travel look easy
Planning & Coordination

The situation

I volunteered to coordinate international school trips for my son's class, including exchange travel to Germany and a later trip to Rome.

The challenge

Around 20–22 students, parents and teachers meant dozens of people, bookings, payments, tickets, travel connections, insurance and individual questions to coordinate.

My response

I became the central coordination point, managing money, travel arrangements, tickets, hotel payments, insurance and communication before and during the trips.

The principle

Good problem solving isn't always about fixing a disaster. Sometimes it means doing enough planning that the disaster never happens.

The result Multiple international trips were successfully delivered, allowing students, parents and teachers to focus on the experience rather than the logistics behind it.
CASE 04
Taking over when everything changed
Leadership Under Pressure

The situation

An unexpected leadership crisis put the organisation under significant pressure. I was away on my days off when the situation emerged and returned immediately.

The challenge

There was no established playbook. Group leadership, owners, banking, legal, finance and internal stakeholders all needed to be brought together.

My response

I stepped into the coordination role, identified who needed to be involved, organised communication and helped turn an unclear situation into concrete actions and decisions.

The human side

I also worked toward a constructive transition, considering the people involved rather than treating the situation purely as a business problem.

What it taught me When there is no clear plan, I don't wait for one to appear. I start building one.

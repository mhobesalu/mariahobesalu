03 — Problem Solving in Action

Real situations where I had to step in, take ownership and figure out what needed to happen next.

CASE 01
The concert that wasn't supposed to happen
Crisis Problem Solving

The situation

A band from Estonia had a concert in Israel when the security situation suddenly changed and flights from Europe to Israel were being cancelled. Their original travel plans were effectively gone.

The challenge

The band needed to get to Israel, but there was no single route or simple solution. Flights were disappearing, plans were changing and people were being affected differently.

My response

I started solving it person by person. I found alternative flights, rebuilt routes as availability changed and kept coordinating the group throughout the process.

When things changed again

Someone became stranded in Warsaw. Another route had to go through Greece instead. Each new problem meant finding a new solution rather than giving up on the original goal.

The result Within roughly 24 hours, I had rebuilt the travel plan and got almost the entire band to Israel. The concert happened.
CASE 02
Taking over when everything changed
Leadership Under Pressure

The situation

An unexpected leadership crisis put the organisation under significant pressure. I was away on my days off when the situation emerged and returned immediately.

The challenge

There was no established playbook. Senior stakeholders from different countries needed to be brought together, including group leadership, owners and CEOs, alongside banking, legal and finance teams.

My response

I stepped into the coordination role, identifying who needed to be involved and bringing international stakeholders together to establish what needed to happen, when and by whom.

The human side

Alongside the business response, I worked toward a constructive transition and tried to handle the situation as humanely as possible for the people involved.

What it taught me When there is no clear plan, I don't wait for one to appear. I start building one.
CASE 03
Turning hundreds of people into one coordinated effort
Purpose & Coordination

The situation

Through my own company, I provided project and organisational services to Wise. Over multiple years, I organised the social-impact track of Wise Summer Days.

The wider event brought together more than 1,000 people, with around 300 choosing to spend their day contributing to different social-impact projects.

The challenge

The 300 participants were distributed across approximately 8–12 organisations and projects, ranging from community and renovation work to activities with children and environmental projects.

In 2020, Wise also approached me to help with Mission Days, an internal conference taking place simultaneously in Tallinn, Budapest and Singapore. Tallinn alone was expected to have 500–600 people.

My responsibility

For Summer Days, I coordinated the organisations and their team leads, participants, transportation, schedules, materials and the practical details needed to make the social-impact day work.

For Mission Days, I was asked to help develop the idea of giving participants an opportunity to spend part of the day doing something useful for others, including exploring whether the concept could work across multiple locations.

Beyond the social track

During Summer Days, I also managed the Co-Team, coordinating the operational infrastructure that kept the event running behind the scenes.

Across these projects, my role was often the same: take a large number of people, different organisations and a moving set of practical details and turn them into something that actually works.

The bigger picture What made these projects meaningful to me was that the problem solving had a purpose. I wasn't just organising people and logistics — I was helping create a situation where hundreds of people could contribute to something bigger than themselves, while the organisations involved benefited from their work.

People & Purpose

I helped organise and lead a team behind a large public demonstration in Tallinn, bringing people together around a shared concern and a common goal.

CASE 05
The December 31st shipment
Time-Critical Operations

The situation

A year-end shipment was at risk. The container was stuck, the shipping company was experiencing problems and the bill of lading was not going to be ready in time.

The challenge

The material needed to leave within the current year. Waiting until January wasn't an acceptable outcome.

My response

I identified the people who could influence each part of the problem, contacted the right parties and coordinated the moving pieces until we found a way forward.

The bigger picture

Month-end and especially year-end were recurring periods of intense operational pressure, requiring quick decisions and constant coordination.

The result I was able to resolve these kinds of issues and keep shipments moving toward the required month-end or year-end deadline.
04 — What I'm looking for

I don't just want
a job.

I want a reason to care.

I've spent much of my career solving problems, coordinating people and making complicated things work.

And I've learned that the work itself doesn't have to be extraordinary for the outcome to be meaningful.

A cause doesn't have to mean a charity. Purpose doesn't have to mean a social-impact organisation. And a common goal doesn't have to mean an event.

It can exist in almost any business, in almost any role.

It can be a team trying to build something better for its customers. It can be an operations team making people's lives easier. It can be a company solving a problem that genuinely matters.

It can be a group of people who trust each other, care about what they're building and want to give their best because they believe in where they're going.

Even the most ordinary work becomes meaningful when you understand who benefits from it and why it matters.

Where I can make the biggest difference

When something is about to become bigger than it currently is.

I think I would be especially useful at a company that is starting to take off and needs someone who can push the next big move.

A company where the product works, the opportunity is real, momentum is building — but the next stage isn't completely figured out yet.

That's where I get excited.

Give me momentum, a difficult goal and a lot of moving pieces, and let me figure out how to push it to the next level.

I like being close to the action. I like figuring out what needs to happen, bringing the right people together, building structure where there isn't enough of it yet, and turning “we should” into “it's done.”

That could mean a startup scaling quickly. It could mean entering a new market, fixing an operational mess, building a team, taking on a complicated project or figuring out what the next move should be.

Not necessarily at the beginning.
Not necessarily at the end.

At the point where someone needs to make the next big thing happen.
How I work best
I don't need a clearly defined role.
I need a clearly defined outcome.

I don't need every step mapped out before I start. In fact, I'm often most useful when the destination is clear but the route isn't.

Give me a problem, an objective, a team that needs direction, or a situation that has become complicated. I need to understand what we're trying to achieve — then I'll figure out what needs to happen.

That can absolutely exist inside a large corporation. I can work within established structures, understand business goals and operate with accountability. What I don't want is for my contribution to be limited to a checklist that ends at the edge of my job description.

I don't see structure and autonomy as opposites.

Large organisations can have enormous complexity, and sometimes that complexity is exactly where I can be useful — connecting teams, navigating stakeholders, fixing gaps between departments and taking ownership of problems that don't fit neatly into one person's job description.

The same applies to a startup or growing company, where the challenge may be figuring out the next move, building the right structure or turning an opportunity into something real.

I don't need to be told exactly what my job is.

Tell me what we're trying to achieve.
I'll figure out how to help us get there.

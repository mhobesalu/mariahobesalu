Real situations where I had to step in, take ownership and figure out what needed to happen next.

CASE 01
The concert that wasn't supposed to happen
Crisis Problem Solving

The situation

A band from Estonia had a concert in Israel when the security situation suddenly changed and flights from Europe to Israel were being cancelled. Their original travel plans were effectively gone.

The challenge

The band needed to get to Israel, but there was no single route or simple solution. Flights were disappearing, plans were changing and people were being affected differently.

My response

I started solving it person by person. I found alternative flights, rebuilt routes as availability changed and kept coordinating the group throughout the process.

When things changed again

Someone became stranded in Warsaw. Another route had to go through Greece instead. Each new problem meant finding a new solution rather than giving up on the original goal.

The result Within roughly 24 hours, I had rebuilt the travel plan and got almost the entire band to Israel. The concert happened.
CASE 02
Taking over when everything changed
Leadership Under Pressure

The situation

An unexpected leadership crisis put the organisation under significant pressure. I was away on my days off when the situation emerged and returned immediately.

The challenge

There was no established playbook. Senior stakeholders from different countries needed to be brought together, including group leadership, owners and CEOs, alongside banking, legal and finance teams.

My response

I stepped into the coordination role, identifying who needed to be involved and bringing international stakeholders together to establish what needed to happen, when and by whom.

The human side

Alongside the business response, I worked toward a constructive transition and tried to handle the situation as humanely as possible for the people involved.

What it taught me When there is no clear plan, I don't wait for one to appear. I start building one.
CASE 03
Turning hundreds of people into one coordinated effort
Large-Scale Coordination

The situation

Through LeMonID, I provided project and organisational services connected to Wise. Over multiple years, I organised the social-impact track of Wise Summer Days.

The wider event brought together large numbers of people, with hundreds choosing to spend their day contributing to different social-impact projects.

The challenge

Participants were distributed across multiple organisations and projects, ranging from community and renovation work to activities with children and environmental projects.

In 2020, Wise also approached me directly to help with Mission Days, an internal conference taking place simultaneously in Tallinn, Budapest and Singapore. Tallinn alone was expected to have 500–600 people.

My responsibility

For Summer Days, I coordinated organisations, team leads, participants, transportation, schedules, materials and the practical details needed to make the social-impact day work.

For Mission Days, I helped develop and coordinate the social-impact element of the conference.

Beyond the social track

During Summer Days, I also managed the Co-Team, coordinating operational infrastructure that kept the event running behind the scenes.

My role was often the same: take a large number of people, different organisations and a moving set of practical details and turn them into something that actually works.

The bigger picture What made these projects meaningful was that the coordination had a purpose. I wasn't just organising people and logistics — I was helping create a situation where hundreds of people could contribute to something bigger than themselves.
CASE 04
The December 31st shipment
Time-Critical Operations

The situation

A year-end shipment was at risk. The container was stuck, the shipping company was experiencing problems and the bill of lading was not going to be ready in time.

The challenge

The material needed to leave within the current year. Waiting until January wasn't an acceptable outcome.

My response

I identified the people who could influence each part of the problem, contacted the right parties and coordinated the moving pieces until we found a way forward.

The bigger picture

Month-end and especially year-end were recurring periods of intense operational pressure, requiring quick decisions and constant coordination.

The result I was able to resolve these kinds of issues and keep shipments moving toward the required month-end or year-end deadline.
04 — What I'm looking for

I don't just want
a job.

I want a reason to care.

I've spent much of my career solving problems, coordinating people and making complicated things work. What I've learned is that the work itself doesn't have to be extraordinary for the outcome to be meaningful.

A cause doesn't have to mean a charity. It can be a company solving a difficult problem, a team building something useful, or an organisation trying to become better at what it does.

What matters to me is understanding why the work matters, caring about the people I'm working with and having enough ownership to make a real difference.

Outcome over role

I don't need a clearly defined role.

I need a meaningful outcome — and the ownership to get there.

I'm often most useful when the destination is clear but the route isn't.

Give me a difficult problem, an objective, a team that needs direction or a situation that has become complicated. I don't need every step mapped out before I start.

I need to understand what we're trying to achieve, why it matters and what I'm empowered to own.

Don't tell me exactly how to get there.

Tell me where we're going, why it matters and let me help figure out how to get there.
And yes — that includes corporations

Structure and autonomy aren't opposites.

I can absolutely see myself working inside a large international organisation. Large organisations can have enormous complexity — and that complexity is sometimes exactly where I can be useful.

I like connecting teams, navigating stakeholders, identifying gaps between departments and taking ownership of problems that don't fit neatly into one person's job description.

I don't need to work somewhere chaotic. I just don't want to be limited by a job description when the situation clearly requires something more.

The same applies to a startup or growing company. The size and structure of the organisation matter less to me than having room to take ownership and make something better.

Where I can make the biggest difference

When something is about to become bigger than it currently is.

I think I would be especially useful at a company that is starting to take off and needs someone who can push the next big move.

A company where the product works, the opportunity is real, momentum is building — but the next stage isn't completely figured out yet.

That's where I get excited.

Give me momentum, a difficult goal and a lot of moving pieces, and let me figure out how to push it to the next level.

I like being close to the action: figuring out what needs to happen, bringing the right people together, building structure where there isn't enough of it yet, and turning “we should” into “it's done.”

Give me a difficult problem.
Give me good people.
Give us something worth achieving.
I'll care about making it happen.
Another way to work together
It doesn't have to be a full-time job.

I'm also open to working on a project or service basis.

I have my own companies through which I can provide services and invoice clients. If you have a difficult project, an operational problem or a transition that needs someone to take ownership of, I can step in and help make it work.

Sometimes you need another permanent person. Sometimes you need someone who can walk into the mess, understand the outcome that matters and take ownership of getting there.

I'm happy to be that person.

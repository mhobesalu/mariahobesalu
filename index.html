03 — Problem Solving in Action

When there is
no obvious answer.

A few examples of how I operate when the situation is complicated, the pressure is real and someone has to take ownership.

CASE 01
The concert that wasn't supposed to happen
Crisis Problem Solving

The situation

A band from Estonia had a concert in Israel when the security situation suddenly changed and flights from Europe were being cancelled.

The challenge

There was no single route forward. Flights were disappearing, plans were changing and different people were getting stuck in different places.

My response

I solved it person by person — rebuilding routes, finding alternative flights and continuously coordinating the group as circumstances changed.

What changed

Someone became stranded in Warsaw. Another route had to go through Greece. Each new problem required another solution without losing sight of the goal.

The result Within roughly 24 hours, I had rebuilt the travel plan and got almost the entire band to Israel. The concert happened.
CASE 02
Taking over when everything changed
Leadership Under Pressure

The situation

An unexpected leadership crisis put the organisation under significant pressure. I was away on my days off when the situation emerged and returned immediately.

The challenge

There was no established playbook. International leadership, owners, CEOs and banking, legal and finance stakeholders all needed to be brought together.

My response

I stepped into the coordination role, identified who needed to be involved and brought the right people together to establish what needed to happen.

The human side

Alongside the business response, I worked toward a constructive transition and tried to handle the situation as humanely as possible.

What it taught me When there is no clear plan, I don't wait for one to appear. I start building one.
CASE 03
Turning hundreds of people into one coordinated effort
Coordination & Purpose

The situation

Through LeMonID, I provided project and organisational services connected to Wise Summer Days over multiple years.

Hundreds of participants chose to spend their day contributing to social-impact projects.

The challenge

Participants had to be distributed across multiple organisations and projects, with transportation, schedules, materials, team leads and practical requirements all working together.

Mission Days

In 2020, Wise approached me directly to help with Mission Days, an internal conference taking place simultaneously in Tallinn, Budapest and Singapore.

My responsibility

I coordinated people, organisations, schedules, transportation and the practical infrastructure needed to turn the concept into something that actually worked.

The bigger picture What mattered to me was not simply organising an event. It was creating a structure where hundreds of people could contribute to something useful and where everyone involved understood the reason behind what they were doing.
CASE 04
The December 31st shipment
Time-Critical Operations

The situation

A year-end shipment was at risk. The container was stuck, the shipping company was experiencing problems and the bill of lading was not going to be ready in time.

The challenge

The material needed to leave within the current year. Waiting until January was not an acceptable outcome.

My response

I identified the people who could influence each part of the problem, contacted the right parties and coordinated the moving pieces until we found a way forward.

The bigger picture

Month-end and especially year-end were recurring periods of intense operational pressure requiring quick decisions and constant coordination.

The result I was able to resolve these kinds of issues and keep shipments moving toward the required month-end or year-end deadline.
04 — What I'm looking for

I don't just want
a job.

I want a reason to care.

I've spent much of my career solving problems, coordinating people and making complicated things work.

And I've learned that the work itself doesn't have to be extraordinary for the outcome to be meaningful.

A cause doesn't have to mean a charity. Purpose doesn't have to mean a social-impact organisation. And a common goal doesn't have to mean an event.

It can exist in almost any business, in almost any role.

It can be a team building something better for its customers. It can be an operations team making people's lives easier. It can be a company solving a problem that genuinely matters.

It can simply be a group of people who trust each other, care about what they're building and want to give their best because they believe in where they're going.

Even ordinary work becomes meaningful when you understand who benefits from it and why it matters.

Where I can make the biggest difference

When something is ready to become bigger.

I think I would be especially useful at a company that is starting to take off and needs someone who can push the next big move.

A company where the product works, the opportunity is real and momentum is building — but the next stage isn't completely figured out yet.

That's where I get excited.

Give me momentum, a difficult goal and a lot of moving pieces, and let me figure out how to push it to the next level.

I like figuring out what needs to happen, bringing the right people together, building structure where there isn't enough of it yet and turning “we should” into “it's done.”

That could mean a startup scaling quickly, entering a new market, fixing an operational mess, building a team, taking on a complicated project or figuring out what the next move should be.

I don't need a perfectly defined role.

I need to understand what we're trying to achieve.

I'm often most useful when the destination is clear but the route isn't.

Give me a problem, an objective, a team that needs direction or a situation that has become complicated. I don't need every step mapped out before I start.

I need a clear outcome — then I'll figure out what needs to happen.

And yes — that includes corporations.

Structure and autonomy aren't opposites.

I don't see large organisations as the opposite of what I'm looking for.

In fact, large organisations can have enormous complexity — and sometimes that complexity is exactly where I can be useful.

Connecting teams. Navigating stakeholders. Fixing gaps between departments. Taking ownership of problems that don't fit neatly into one person's job description.

I can work within structure. I just don't want the structure to become a reason nobody takes ownership.

That's what I'm looking for.

I want to be part of something where people are working toward a common goal, rather than simply completing tasks.

Where I can bring people together, build trust and help a team become something more than a collection of individuals.

Where solving a difficult problem isn't just about getting it off someone's desk — it's about creating a better outcome for someone.

Where I can look at the work I'm doing and understand:
